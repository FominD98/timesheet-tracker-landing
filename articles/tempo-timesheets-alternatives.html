Tempo Timesheets alternatives: an honest comparison

Before you replace Tempo, figure out whether your problem is the ledger, the price, or the fact that nobody remembers what they did on Tuesday.

By AI Timesheet team · October 19, 2026 · 8 min read
Short answer
  • Tempo alternatives fall into three groups: Jira add-ons that replace the ledger, desktop auto-trackers that fill it, and general trackers that sync to Jira.
  • If your pain is price or admin overhead, look at other Jira apps or native Jira time tracking. If your pain is remembering what you did, a new ledger won't fix it.
  • You can keep Tempo and still stop typing: a desktop tracker that posts native Jira worklogs feeds Tempo without any extra integration.
  • Agencies billing clients usually need a different tool than a 200-person engineering org. Decide by role, not by feature list.

Why teams look for a Tempo alternative

Tempo Timesheets is one of the most established time tracking apps for Jira, and for many organizations it works fine. When people look for a Tempo Timesheets alternative, it is rarely because Tempo is broken. In our experience, there are four reasons.

1. Pricing scales with your whole Jira tier

Like most Atlassian Marketplace apps, Tempo uses per-user pricing tied to your whole Jira user tier, so you typically pay for every licensed Jira user, not only those who log time. If many seats belong to people who never touch a timesheet, renewal stings. Check current tiers on the Marketplace listing before building a business case.

2. The real pain is remembering, not recording

This is the one we hear most. Tempo is the ledger: a well-organized place to store hours against issues, accounts and teams. It is not the memory. On Friday afternoon, the developer staring at an empty week still has to reconstruct which tickets ate their Wednesday. Another ledger just moves the empty grid to a different screen.

3. Admin overhead

Accounts, approvals, work attributes, teams, reports: Tempo is powerful because it is configurable, and configuration needs an owner. Smaller teams often use a fraction of it and still maintain all of it.

4. You just need native Jira worklogs

Some teams only need hours on issues. Jira does that out of the box. If nobody reads the Tempo reports, the honest alternative might be no add-on at all.

Three kinds of alternatives (and why it matters)

Most "Tempo alternatives" lists mix tools that solve different problems. Sort them first:

  • (a) Jira add-ons that replace the ledger. Clockwork, 7pace Timetracker, the various Timetracker-style Marketplace apps, and native Jira time tracking. Same job as Tempo: store and report hours in Jira.
  • (b) Desktop auto-trackers that fill the ledger. Memtime, Timely, Timesheet Tracker. They watch your own activity and help you turn it into entries, so you don't have to remember.
  • (c) General trackers with Jira integrations. Toggl Track, Clockify. A standalone time database with timers and reports, connected to Jira.
If you are replacing Tempo because of cost or admin load, you are shopping in group (a). If you are replacing it because timesheets are always late and inaccurate, you are probably shopping in group (b), and you may not need to replace Tempo at all.

Jira add-ons that replace the ledger

Native Jira time tracking

What it is: the built-in "Log work" feature on every Jira issue, with original estimate, time spent and remaining estimate. Best for: teams that only need hours per issue and are happy with basic reporting. Watch-outs: reporting is thin, there is no real weekly timesheet view, and no concept of billing accounts. Our guide on how to log time in Jira covers what it can and can't do.

Clockwork

What it is: a Jira time tracking app by HeroCoders. As we understand it, it offers timesheets and reports plus automatic timers that start and stop based on issue status changes. Best for: teams that want a lighter Tempo-like experience inside Jira. Watch-outs: status-based timers only measure time while an issue sits in a status, which is not the same as time you actually worked on it; review the feature set and per-user pricing on the Atlassian Marketplace, and check current tiers.

7pace Timetracker

What it is: a time tracker best known for Azure DevOps, which has also been offered for Jira. Best for: organizations that run work across Azure DevOps and want one familiar tracker. Watch-outs: check current availability and roadmap for the Jira version before committing; product lines in this space change.

Timetracker-style Marketplace apps

What they are: several Marketplace apps offer timesheets, worklog reports and approvals on top of Jira worklogs. Best for: Tempo-like reporting at a different price or with simpler admin. Watch-outs: they are still the ledger. Your people will still have to remember and type.

Desktop auto-trackers that fill the ledger

These tools record what you work on and help turn that history into entries, which solves the memory problem directly. See our longer comparison of automatic time tracking software for Windows.

Memtime

What it is: a desktop app that records a private timeline of your apps and documents so you can assign blocks to projects. Best for: individuals and consultants who want a detailed, private activity log. Watch-outs: pricing is roughly $14–30 per user per month depending on plan, and it is not in the Microsoft Store, which matters in some managed IT environments.

Timely

What it is: an automatic time tracker that captures activity and suggests time entries, with team features on higher plans. Best for: agencies and professional services teams that bill by the hour. Watch-outs: roughly $11–28 per user per month; check how its Jira connection fits your worklog workflow before rolling it out.

Timesheet Tracker

What it is: our own AI timesheet for Jira, a Windows desktop app that lives in the tray and records the active app, window title, browser tab domain and idle/lock time, all stored locally. It finds Jira keys like ABC-123 in window titles, URLs and git branch names on your machine, without AI. When you click "Sort my day," AI assigns the remaining blocks to Jira tasks and drafts one worklog text per task. You review, confirm, and the worklogs go to Jira Cloud or Jira Server/Data Center, with no duplicates, or out to CSV. Best for: people who log their own time in Jira and are tired of reconstructing their week. Watch-outs: it is new: a free download from the Microsoft Store with a 14-day free trial of Pro, then $9.99 a month or $79 a year. It is Windows-only, built for individuals, with no manager reports by design.

General trackers with Jira integrations

Toggl Track

What it is: a popular timer-based tracker with a free plan, browser extensions and a Jira integration. Best for: mixed teams where only some work lives in Jira. Watch-outs: your time lives in Toggl first; confirm whether entries land as native Jira worklogs or only in Toggl's own reports.

Clockify

What it is: a time tracker with a free plan and paid tiers for approvals, billing and more. Best for: budget-conscious teams and agencies that need client billing outside Jira. Watch-outs: same as Toggl: check exactly how the Jira sync behaves, and remember that timers still depend on people starting and stopping them.

You can keep Tempo and still stop typing

Here is the nuance most comparisons miss: Tempo shows Jira worklogs. Work logged on an issue through Jira's own interface or API appears in Tempo's timesheets and reports. So you don't have to choose between "keep Tempo" and "get automatic time tracking."

A desktop tracker that drafts worklogs and posts them as native Jira worklogs fills Tempo's ledger for you. Finance keeps its reports, admins keep approvals, and the person logging time reviews a draft instead of rebuilding a week from memory. In our experience, that is where most of the frustration with Tempo actually lives, and it is the cheapest part to fix.

Timesheet Tracker posts native Jira worklogs today, which Tempo can display. A direct Tempo integration (for Tempo-specific fields like accounts or work attributes) is planned but not available yet. If your Tempo setup requires those fields on every entry, test the workflow before relying on it.

Decision guide: which alternative fits you

If you're the person who logs time

You probably don't control which ledger your company uses, and you don't need to. You need a reliable record of your own day. Pick a desktop auto-tracker that respects your privacy, writes to whatever your team uses, and lets you review entries before they are posted. Timesheet Tracker, for example, keeps activity local and only sends masked window titles, app names, domains and durations when you click the AI button, never full URLs, page content, screenshots, keystrokes or your Jira token. There is also a "No AI" mode.

If you're the admin choosing for 200 people

List what you actually use in Tempo today. If the list is short, compare group (a) apps or plain native Jira, and model per-user pricing across your full Jira tier. If it is long, keep Tempo and attack accuracy instead: late, guessed timesheets are a data quality problem no ledger solves. In our experience, adding screenshots or keystroke monitoring makes people resent the timesheet more, not fill it better.

If you're an agency billing clients

You care about billable time, client rates and invoices. A general tracker such as Clockify or Toggl Track, or an auto-tracker with billing features such as Timely, may fit better than any Jira-native app, especially if some client work never touches Jira.

The bottom line

Replacing Tempo makes sense when price or admin load no longer matches its value. But if the complaint is "nobody fills it in on time," the fix is upstream: capture the day automatically, let people review it, and post ordinary Jira worklogs. That works with Tempo, native Jira and most other ledgers. If you are curious how far AI can take that, read can AI fill your timesheet?
